# 3. Ethics and Social Impact Assessment

## 3.1. Ethical Vision and Guiding Principles

The proposed FEMConnect platform aims to use artificial intelligence (AI) to improve inclusive employment support provided by Fundació Esclerosi Múltiple (FEM) through its FEMTalent service. Its ethical vision is based on a central principle: **technology should adapt employment opportunities and support services to people, rather than reducing people to the limitations of an algorithm.**

People with Multiple Sclerosis (MS) and other disabilities may encounter barriers during recruitment, professional development and workplace participation. These barriers can arise from inaccessible recruitment procedures, inflexible job requirements, limited awareness of reasonable workplace adaptations, and administrative constraints within employment-support services. AI could help address some of these challenges, but it could also reproduce or reinforce existing inequalities if its design and use are not carefully controlled.

FEMConnect should therefore be developed as a human-centred decision-support ecosystem. It should support participants, employment advisers and employers without replacing professional judgement or the autonomy of individuals seeking employment.

The following principles should guide the design, implementation and future operation of the platform:

**Human dignity and individual autonomy.** Every participant must be treated as an individual with their own professional aspirations, skills, preferences and circumstances. AI-generated suggestions must not define a person's employability, professional potential or future opportunities.

**Equality and non-discrimination.** The platform must not disadvantage individuals because of disability, health-related circumstances, age, gender or other protected characteristics. Its purpose should be to identify and reduce barriers in employment processes, not to reproduce them through automated analysis.

**Human oversight and accountability.** AI-generated information and recommendations must remain subject to meaningful human review. Employment advisers should be able to correct, modify, reject or override system outputs, and responsibility for decisions must remain with the appropriate human decision-makers.

**Privacy and data protection.** The platform should collect and process only the information necessary for its defined purposes. Health-related information and information about functional support needs must receive particularly careful protection.

**Transparency and explainability.** Participants and advisers should understand when AI is being used, what information it considers, what its outputs mean and what limitations apply. Recommendations should be supported by understandable explanations and identifiable evidence wherever possible.

**Accessibility and inclusion.** The platform must be designed to accommodate different abilities, interaction preferences and accessibility requirements. Accessibility should be a core design condition rather than an optional feature added after development.

**Fairness and continuous improvement.** The system should be regularly evaluated for unequal performance, inaccurate recommendations and unintended consequences. Feedback from participants and advisers should contribute to continuous improvement.

These principles establish the ethical foundation for the four proposed FEMConnect modules: the Administrative Assistant for Employment Advisers, the Job Vacancy Barrier Analyser, the Inclusive Job and Workplace-Adaptation Matcher, and the Training and Career Pathway Generator.

## 3.2. Social Impact and Expected Benefits

The expected social impact of FEMConnect extends beyond administrative efficiency. Its principal objective is to strengthen the capacity of FEMTalent to provide accessible, personalised and inclusive employment support.

The anticipated benefits concern participants, employment advisers, employers and the organisation as a whole. These benefits are expected outcomes of the proposed design and must be validated through user involvement and a future pilot.

### 3.2.1. Improving Equal Employment Opportunities

People with disabilities may encounter recruitment requirements that do not adequately distinguish between essential job functions and conventional or unnecessarily restrictive working practices. For example, a vacancy may require a particular working arrangement, physical presence or communication method even when alternative arrangements could enable the essential tasks to be performed.

The Job Vacancy Barrier Analyser is intended to help identify potentially restrictive requirements and suggest more inclusive alternatives. It could encourage employers to reconsider whether particular requirements are genuinely necessary and whether reasonable workplace adaptations could be considered.

This may contribute to more accessible recruitment processes and a broader range of employment opportunities for people with disabilities.

However, the platform must not assume that every requirement is discriminatory or that every proposed adaptation is feasible in every workplace. The purpose of the analysis is to identify questions for human review and constructive discussion with employers.

### 3.2.2. Strengthening Participant Autonomy and Empowerment

Employment support should enable participants to make informed decisions about their professional futures. It should not impose career paths or define individuals according to their medical conditions.

FEMConnect could support participant autonomy by:

- Presenting employment and training opportunities aligned with individual goals and preferences.
- Helping participants understand potential barriers and possible workplace adaptations.
- Allowing individuals to review and correct information held in their profiles.
- Providing explanations for suggested opportunities and training pathways.
- Allowing participants to decide what information they wish to share and for what purpose, subject to applicable legal requirements.

The platform should encourage a collaborative process in which participants remain active decision-makers rather than passive recipients of automated recommendations.

### 3.2.3. Reducing Administrative Workload for Employment Advisers

Employment advisers perform a range of activities, including participant interviews, profile preparation, documentation, vacancy analysis, follow-up communication and coordination with employers.

These responsibilities can create significant administrative demands and reduce the time available for direct participant support.

The Administrative Assistant could help reduce repetitive documentation tasks through speech-to-text transcription, information extraction, draft profile creation, interview summaries and follow-up task suggestions.

The expected social benefit is not simply faster administration. It is the possibility of reallocating part of the time saved towards individual guidance, participant engagement and employer collaboration.

AI-generated documentation must remain a draft requiring adviser validation. Reducing administrative work must not come at the expense of accuracy, confidentiality or the quality of personal support.

### 3.2.4. Increasing Employer Awareness and Inclusive Recruitment Practices

Employers may not always recognise how vacancy wording, recruitment procedures or workplace arrangements can create unnecessary barriers.

The Vacancy Barrier Analyser could provide structured feedback on potentially restrictive requirements and suggest alternative wording or questions for further consideration.

This may help employers:

- Distinguish essential job requirements from preferences or conventional practices.
- Consider reasonable adjustments and alternative working arrangements.
- Improve the accessibility of vacancy descriptions.
- Develop a better understanding of inclusive recruitment.
- Engage more constructively with FEMTalent employment advisers.

The platform should act as an advisory resource rather than an automated certification mechanism. It should not label an employer or vacancy as discriminatory solely on the basis of an AI-generated assessment.

### 3.2.5. Supporting Personalised Training and Career Development

Access to employment also depends on opportunities for professional development, skills acquisition and career progression.

The Training and Career Pathway Generator could help identify relevant training options based on a participant's skills, interests, professional objectives and preferred learning conditions.

Its recommendations could support individuals who wish to change professional direction, update their skills or explore new employment opportunities.

The module should present several possible pathways where appropriate, explain the reasons behind its suggestions and allow participants and advisers to consider alternatives. It must not restrict access to training or assume that a person's disability limits their professional aspirations.

### 3.2.6. Strengthening FEMTalent's Organisational Capacity

At organisational level, the proposed ecosystem could improve the consistency of documentation, support the analysis of employment barriers and provide more structured information for service evaluation.

Aggregated and appropriately anonymised information could help FEMTalent identify recurring barriers in vacancies, common training needs and areas where employer engagement may be beneficial.

These insights could support service planning and continuous improvement. They should not be used to create unnecessary individual profiles or to make unsupported assumptions about the employment potential of particular groups.

## 3.3. Ethical Risks and Challenges

Although FEMConnect offers potential benefits, its use introduces ethical risks that must be considered throughout the system lifecycle.

### 3.3.1. Algorithmic Bias and Discrimination

AI systems can reproduce biases present in their training data, reference material, design assumptions or operational environment.

For example, if a matching system learns from historical recruitment outcomes, it may reproduce patterns in which people with disabilities were underrepresented in particular occupations. Similarly, vacancy analysis may incorrectly treat conventional physical or organisational requirements as essential because they appear frequently in existing job descriptions.

Potential consequences include:

- Recommending fewer opportunities to certain participants.
- Overemphasising limitations rather than skills and preferences.
- Treating missing information as evidence of incompatibility.
- Reinforcing historical employment inequalities.
- Producing different levels of recommendation quality for different participant groups.

The platform should not calculate a general employability score or automatically rank candidates. It should instead identify relevant dimensions of a potential match, including skills, preferences, possible adaptations and missing information.

Fairness testing should examine both the quality of outputs and their practical effects on participants.

### 3.3.2. Privacy and Processing of Sensitive Personal Data

The platform may process personal information such as employment histories, qualifications, professional aspirations, interview notes, accessibility preferences and workplace support requirements.

Some information about functional limitations or support needs may reveal or allow inferences about a person's health or disability. Such information may therefore constitute special-category personal data under the GDPR, even when an explicit medical diagnosis is not recorded.

Potential risks include:

- Unauthorised access to participant profiles.
- Disclosure of health-related information to employers.
- Retention of interview recordings or transcripts beyond their necessary purpose.
- Use of personal information for purposes different from those originally explained.
- Exposure of sensitive information through external AI services.
- Re-identification of individuals in supposedly anonymised reports.

The system should apply data minimisation, purpose limitation, access controls, appropriate retention periods and secure processing arrangements. The organisation must establish an appropriate legal basis under Article 6 of the GDPR and, where special-category data is involved, a valid condition under Article 9.

Information about a participant's support needs should not automatically be disclosed to an employer. Any disclosure must be assessed against its specific purpose, the participant's rights and the applicable legal requirements.

### 3.3.3. Lack of Transparency and Explainability

AI-generated outputs may be difficult to understand, particularly when they are produced by complex language models or semantic matching systems.

If participants and advisers cannot understand why a vacancy or training option has been recommended, they may be unable to identify errors or challenge inappropriate suggestions.

The platform should therefore provide explanations in accessible language. For example, a matching result could identify:

- Which professional skills correspond to the vacancy.
- Which stated preferences are satisfied.
- Which working conditions may require further discussion.
- Which potential adaptations could be considered.
- Which relevant information is missing or uncertain.

The system should distinguish clearly between information extracted from a source, an AI-generated interpretation and a recommendation requiring human assessment.

It must not present uncertain or inferred information as established fact.

### 3.3.4. Over-Reliance on AI and Loss of Human Judgement

Employment advisers may gradually rely too heavily on automated summaries, vacancy assessments or matching recommendations, particularly when these tools appear consistent and authoritative.

This creates a risk of automation bias, in which users accept AI-generated outputs without sufficiently questioning their accuracy.

For example, an adviser might overlook a suitable opportunity because the matching system identifies a potential incompatibility, even though a reasonable workplace adaptation could resolve the issue.

Meaningful human oversight requires more than a final approval button. Advisers must have sufficient information, authority, time and training to assess system outputs independently.

Participants must also have opportunities to express disagreement and request reconsideration.

### 3.3.5. Inaccurate or Incomplete Information

AI systems can produce incorrect transcriptions, omit relevant details, misinterpret statements or generate plausible but unsupported conclusions.

In the Administrative Assistant, a transcription error could change the meaning of a participant's statement. An incorrect profile summary could then influence later matching or training recommendations.

Similarly, a vacancy analyser could misinterpret ambiguous job requirements or generate recommendations that are not supported by the original vacancy text.

The platform should preserve links to relevant source information, clearly identify uncertainty and allow users to correct inaccurate records. AI-generated content must not silently become verified participant data.

### 3.3.6. Digital Exclusion and Unequal Accessibility

An AI-based service may unintentionally exclude individuals if its interface, interaction methods or generated content are inaccessible.

Potential barriers include complex navigation, small text, insufficient contrast, inaccessible forms, speech-recognition errors, poorly structured screen-reader output and excessive reliance on voice interaction.

Accessibility requirements may differ substantially between individuals, including among people with the same diagnosis. The platform must therefore support configurable interaction options and avoid assumptions about individual abilities.

Digital tools should complement, not replace, accessible human support.

### 3.3.7. Confidentiality and Trust

Participants may share personal information with FEMTalent because they trust the organisation to use it in their interests.

If information is processed by external AI providers, shared with employers or reused for purposes that participants do not understand, that trust may be undermined.

The organisation should explain how AI services are used, which categories of information are processed, who can access the information and how long it is retained.

External AI providers should be assessed for their data-protection, confidentiality, security and contractual arrangements. Sensitive participant information should not be entered into public AI services without an approved and legally compliant processing arrangement.

## 3.4. Legal and Regulatory Framework

FEMConnect should be designed in accordance with the applicable European and Spanish legal framework, particularly provisions concerning disability rights, personal data protection, equality and artificial intelligence.

The following instruments provide the principal reference points for the proposed solution. Their application must be assessed against the platform's final intended purposes, technical design and operating arrangements.

### 3.4.1. United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities

The United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) establishes an international framework for protecting the rights and participation of persons with disabilities.

Article 27, concerning work and employment, recognises the right of persons with disabilities to work on an equal basis with others. It refers to employment opportunities in labour markets and working environments that are open, inclusive and accessible.

It also addresses discrimination in recruitment and employment, equal opportunities, career development and the provision of reasonable accommodation.

The proposed FEMConnect platform is consistent with these objectives because it seeks to identify employment barriers, support inclusive vacancy practices and improve access to professional development.

The CRPD provides an important rights-based foundation: the purpose of the platform should be to improve access to employment, not to determine which individuals are sufficiently employable.

### 3.4.2. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the General Data Protection Regulation, governs the processing of personal data within the European Union.

Several provisions are particularly relevant to FEMConnect:

- **Article 5 – Principles relating to processing:** Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently, collected for specified purposes, limited to what is necessary, kept accurate and retained only for an appropriate period. Integrity and confidentiality must also be ensured.
- **Article 6 – Lawfulness of processing:** The organisation must identify a valid legal basis for each processing purpose.
- **Article 9 – Special categories of personal data:** Data concerning health is subject to additional restrictions. A valid Article 9 condition must be identified where such data is processed.
- **Articles 12–14 – Transparency and information:** Participants must receive clear information about the processing of their personal data.
- **Article 22 – Automated individual decision-making:** The organisation must assess whether any proposed processing involves decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal or similarly significant effects, including profiling, and ensure that the relevant safeguards and conditions are respected.
- **Article 25 – Data protection by design and by default:** Privacy safeguards should be incorporated into the platform from the design stage.
- **Article 32 – Security of processing:** Appropriate technical and organisational security measures must be implemented.
- **Article 35 – Data protection impact assessment:** A DPIA must be carried out where the proposed processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms.

For FEMConnect, a DPIA should be considered at an early design stage and completed where required. Given the potential processing of health-related information, the use of AI and the context of employment support, a formal assessment is particularly important.

The platform should also establish clear responsibilities between FEM, FEMTalent, technology suppliers and any other organisations involved in processing personal data.

### 3.4.3. European Union Artificial Intelligence Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act), establishes harmonised rules for the development, placing on the market and use of AI systems in the European Union.

The Act adopts a risk-based approach and imposes different requirements depending on the intended purpose and risk classification of an AI system.

Employment-related AI is particularly relevant because Annex III includes certain systems intended for recruitment and selection, including systems used to analyse and filter job applications or evaluate candidates.

The proposed Inclusive Job and Workplace-Adaptation Matcher therefore requires particular attention. Its classification will depend on its actual intended purpose and operation. If it is used to evaluate, filter or materially influence recruitment or selection decisions, it may fall within the high-risk category.

Other modules should also be assessed individually. An administrative tool that performs transcription or prepares draft summaries may have a different classification from a system that materially influences employment decisions. A preparatory or narrow procedural function does not automatically make a system high-risk or exempt: the relevant conditions and exceptions in Article 6 must be assessed, including the rules concerning profiling.

Where a module is classified as high-risk, applicable requirements may include risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity. The responsibilities of the provider and deployer must also be identified.

The project should therefore avoid assuming that all modules have the same regulatory status. A documented classification assessment should be completed before deployment and revisited if the intended purpose or functionality changes.

### 3.4.4. Equality, Non-Discrimination and Reasonable Accommodation

European and Spanish equality and disability legislation provides additional protection against discrimination and supports the right to reasonable accommodation in employment.

The platform should be aligned with the principle that workplace requirements and recruitment practices must be considered in their actual context. A requirement that appears restrictive should be assessed in relation to the essential functions of the job and the possibility of reasonable accommodation.

The Vacancy Barrier Analyser should support this assessment by identifying potentially problematic wording and proposing alternatives for human review. It must not make definitive legal determinations or replace advice from qualified professionals.

FEM should also review the applicable Spanish legal framework and relevant employment-sector requirements before operational deployment.

### 3.4.5. Implications for FEMConnect

The legal framework leads to several practical requirements:

1. Establish a clear legal basis and defined purpose for each personal-data processing activity.
2. Identify and protect any health-related or other special-category information.
3. Assess the AI Act classification of each module according to its intended purpose.
4. Conduct a DPIA where the GDPR requires one.
5. Provide accessible privacy information and appropriate mechanisms for exercising data-subject rights.
6. Maintain appropriate security, access control, retention and supplier-management procedures.
7. Ensure that AI does not introduce discriminatory employment practices or replace legally required human judgement.
8. Obtain legal and data-protection review before the platform is deployed.

### 3.4.6. Regulatory Interpretation and Limitations

This section provides a project-level overview rather than a formal legal opinion. The final legal classification of each module cannot be determined from a conceptual description alone.

In particular, the AI Act classification depends on the actual intended purpose, system functionality and use in practice. The GDPR assessment depends on the specific data flows, legal bases, processing operations and relationships between the parties.

FEM should therefore involve its Data Protection Officer or qualified data-protection adviser, together with appropriate legal expertise, before any real participant data is processed.

## 3.5. Ethical Risk Assessment Across the Four Platform Modules

The ethical risks identified above affect the four FEMConnect modules in different ways. A module-specific assessment helps translate general principles into concrete design and operational requirements.

The following assessment identifies the principal risks, potential impacts and proposed safeguards. It represents an initial qualitative assessment; the risks and their severity should be validated through a formal risk-assessment process before deployment.

### 3.5.1. Administrative Assistant for Employment Advisers

**Purpose:** Support advisers with transcription, structured information extraction, participant-profile drafting, interview summaries and follow-up documentation.

**Main ethical risks:**

- Transcription errors or inaccurate interpretation of participant statements.
- Accidental inclusion of unnecessary health-related or sensitive information.
- Disclosure of confidential information through external AI services.
- AI-generated summaries omitting relevant context or introducing unsupported conclusions.
- Advisers relying on draft content without adequate verification.

**Potential impact:** Inaccurate information could become part of a participant's record and influence subsequent guidance, matching or training recommendations. A privacy incident could also damage trust between participants and FEMTalent.

**Required safeguards:**

- Treat all AI-generated content as an unverified draft.
- Require adviser review and approval before information becomes part of an official participant record.
- Preserve access to original notes or recordings where retention is justified and authorised.
- Minimise the amount of personal and health-related information processed.
- Use approved AI services with appropriate contractual, confidentiality and security arrangements.
- Provide mechanisms to correct or delete inaccurate information where legally appropriate.
- Apply role-based access controls and retention rules.

**Residual concern:** Human validation reduces the risk of incorrect documentation but cannot eliminate it. Advisers need sufficient time and appropriate training to perform meaningful checks.

### 3.5.2. Job Vacancy Barrier Analyser

**Purpose:** Analyse vacancy descriptions to identify potentially restrictive or non-inclusive requirements and suggest more accessible wording or employer-advisory points.

**Main ethical risks:**

- Incorrectly identifying legitimate job requirements as discriminatory.
- Failing to recognise subtle or indirect barriers.
- Producing generic or impractical adaptation suggestions.
- Presenting AI-generated interpretations as definitive legal conclusions.
- Introducing bias through the examples or reference material used by the system.

**Potential impact:** Employers may receive misleading advice, while genuine barriers may remain undetected. Incorrect assessments could undermine the credibility of FEMTalent and its relationships with employers.

**Required safeguards:**

- Link each identified concern to the relevant vacancy text.
- Distinguish between extracted requirements, possible barriers and suggested improvements.
- Use qualified human review before sending advisory reports to employers.
- Avoid automatic labels such as "discriminatory employer" or "illegal vacancy".
- Explain uncertainty and identify where additional information is required.
- Validate suggestions against relevant employment and accessibility guidance.
- Include employer feedback in the evaluation process.

**Residual concern:** Vacancy descriptions may not accurately reflect actual working conditions. Further discussion with employers may be necessary before drawing conclusions.

### 3.5.3. Inclusive Job and Workplace-Adaptation Matcher

**Purpose:** Compare validated participant profiles with job vacancies, identify areas of compatibility and highlight potential workplace adaptations or missing information.

**Main ethical risks:**

- Discriminatory matching outcomes.
- Treating disability or support needs as automatic indicators of incompatibility.
- Excluding opportunities because of missing or incomplete information.
- Overemphasising limitations instead of skills and professional preferences.
- Creating an implicit candidate ranking or employability score.
- Influencing recruitment or selection decisions without adequate oversight.

**Potential impact:** This module presents the most significant ethical concern because its outputs could influence access to employment opportunities. Inappropriate recommendations could narrow participants' options or reinforce existing employment inequalities.

**Required safeguards:**

- Do not generate a scalar employability score or automatic candidate ranking.
- Do not automatically reject or exclude participants from vacancies.
- Present results using descriptive categories such as:
  - **Supports fit:** Available information indicates alignment with relevant job requirements and preferences.
  - **Needs adaptation:** One or more working conditions may require discussion or possible accommodation.
  - **Unknown:** There is insufficient information to make a reliable assessment.
- Never interpret missing information as a negative characteristic.
- Separate professional skills and experience from functional support requirements.
- Explain the evidence behind each identified match or potential barrier.
- Allow participants and advisers to correct profile information.
- Require meaningful human assessment before any consequential decision.
- Assess the module's AI Act classification before deployment, particularly if it is used in recruitment or selection.

**Residual concern:** Even when presented as advisory, matching outputs may influence human decisions. Monitoring must therefore examine how advisers actually use the recommendations, not only whether the system technically permits human override.

### 3.5.4. Training and Career Pathway Generator

**Purpose:** Suggest training opportunities and career-development pathways based on participants' professional objectives, skills, preferences and learning requirements.

**Main ethical risks:**

- Recommending overly restrictive career pathways based on assumptions about disability.
- Reproducing stereotypes in training recommendations.
- Prioritising certain courses because of incomplete or biased information.
- Ignoring participant aspirations or previous experience.
- Presenting suggestions as mandatory or definitive.

**Potential impact:** Participants may be discouraged from pursuing professional opportunities or may receive unnecessarily narrow recommendations.

**Required safeguards:**

- Base recommendations on participant-defined goals and validated information.
- Present multiple options where possible.
- Explain why each pathway is suggested.
- Allow participants to reject or modify recommendations.
- Avoid assumptions that a particular diagnosis determines career suitability.
- Include professional aspirations and transferable skills in the recommendation process.
- Ensure that access to training is not automatically restricted by AI outputs.
- Require adviser review when recommendations may materially influence an individual's development plan.

**Residual concern:** Training recommendations may still reinforce existing inequalities if the available training catalogue or historical participation data is unrepresentative. Periodic review of recommendation diversity is therefore necessary.

| Module                                | Principal ethical risk                         | Potential impact                                  | Priority safeguard                                      |

## 3.6. Ethical Safeguards and Responsible AI Governance

Ethical principles must be translated into technical and organisational controls. FEMConnect should therefore incorporate responsible AI governance throughout its lifecycle, from initial design and data collection to deployment, monitoring and eventual retirement.

### 3.6.1. Human-in-the-Loop Governance

Human oversight should be integrated into all four modules.

Employment advisers should be able to:

- Review AI-generated information before it is stored or used.
- Correct inaccurate or incomplete outputs.
- Request additional information when an assessment is uncertain.
- Modify or reject recommendations.
- Record significant overrides and the reasons for them.
- Escalate complex cases for specialist review.

The system should not make consequential employment decisions on behalf of FEMTalent or employers. AI outputs should support professional assessment, not replace it.

Participants should also be able to question information in their profiles, express disagreement with recommendations and participate in decisions concerning their employment goals.

### 3.6.2. Data Governance and Privacy by Design

A data-governance framework should define which information is collected, why it is required, who may access it and how long it is retained.

Key requirements include:

- Collect only information necessary for the relevant service.
- Separate sensitive support-needs information from general professional information where appropriate.
- Apply role-based access controls.
- Encrypt data in transit and at rest where appropriate.
- Establish secure authentication and access-monitoring procedures.
- Define retention and deletion rules.
- Document data-sharing arrangements with external suppliers.
- Avoid using participant information to train external or general-purpose AI models without a separate, justified and legally compliant arrangement.

Where possible, analytical reporting should use aggregated or anonymised information. Pseudonymised information remains personal data under the GDPR and must continue to be protected.

### 3.6.3. Transparency and Explainability

The platform should provide clear explanations of AI-supported operations.

For participants, this means explaining:

- When AI is being used.
- What information is processed.
- Why information is requested.
- How recommendations are generated at a meaningful level.
- What the system cannot reliably determine.
- How to request correction or human review.

For advisers, the platform should provide supporting evidence, confidence or uncertainty indicators where meaningful, and clear distinctions between source information and AI-generated interpretation.

Explanations should be understandable and accessible rather than relying on technical descriptions of algorithms.

### 3.6.4. Fairness Monitoring and Bias Mitigation

Fairness should be assessed throughout the system lifecycle.

The organisation should:

- Review the representativeness and quality of relevant datasets.
- Examine whether certain groups receive systematically different recommendation quality.
- Test whether missing information creates unintended disadvantages.
- Review vacancy analysis across different job sectors and working arrangements.
- Monitor adviser overrides and recurring errors.
- Involve participants and disability organisations in identifying potential harms.
- Document identified limitations and corrective actions.

Any fairness analysis involving sensitive personal data must itself be designed and conducted in accordance with applicable data-protection law.

### 3.6.5. Auditability and Accountability

Significant AI-supported activities should be traceable.

Depending on the module and applicable legal requirements, the platform should record:

- The relevant input information and source references.
- The AI-generated output.
- The model or service version used.
- The date and context of processing.
- The adviser review or approval.
- Significant corrections or overrides.
- Relevant complaints, incidents and corrective actions.

Audit records should be proportionate to the purpose and should not create unnecessary repositories of sensitive information.

Organisational responsibilities must be defined clearly. FEM should identify who is responsible for system ownership, data protection, technical management, user training, incident handling and periodic review.

### 3.6.6. User Training and Responsible Use

Advisers and other authorised users should receive training on:

- AI capabilities and limitations.
- Common errors and hallucinations in generative AI.
- Data protection and confidentiality.
- Bias and discriminatory outcomes.
- Human oversight and escalation procedures.
- Accessible communication.
- Appropriate interpretation of recommendations.

Participants should receive accessible information about the role of AI in the service and how they can exercise their rights.

### 3.6.7. Incident Management and Continuous Improvement

FEM should establish a procedure for reporting and investigating incidents, including inaccurate recommendations, inappropriate disclosure, security breaches and potentially discriminatory outcomes.

The procedure should define how incidents are assessed, escalated, documented and corrected. Where personal-data breaches occur, the applicable GDPR notification obligations must be followed.

The platform should be reviewed periodically, and significant changes to its functionality, data sources or intended purpose should trigger renewed ethical, technical and legal assessment.

## 3.7. Accessibility and Inclusive User Experience

Accessibility is a fundamental requirement for FEMConnect because the platform is intended to support individuals who may experience different physical, cognitive, sensory or communication-related barriers.

The system should not assume that all people with MS or other disabilities have the same accessibility needs. Individual requirements may differ and may change over time.

### 3.7.1. Accessible Interface Design

The user interface should follow recognised accessibility principles, including the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), with WCAG 2.2 Level AA serving as a proposed design target.

Relevant requirements include:

- Clear and consistent navigation.
- Sufficient colour contrast.
- Adjustable text size and readable typography.
- Keyboard-accessible controls.
- Screen-reader-compatible content and forms.
- Meaningful headings and labels.
- Clear error messages and instructions.
- Avoidance of unnecessary time limits.
- Responsive layouts across supported devices.

Accessibility testing should involve people with different abilities and assistive technologies.

### 3.7.2. Configurable Interaction Options

The platform should offer configurable accessibility options where appropriate, including:

- Speech-to-text for users who prefer or require voice input.
- Text-to-speech for users who benefit from audio presentation.
- Captions and transcripts for audio or video content.
- Keyboard navigation and alternative input methods.
- High-contrast display options.
- Simplified interface layouts.
- Adjustable text size and spacing.
- Clear, plain-language explanations.

These options should be configurable rather than automatically assigned based on a participant's diagnosis.

### 3.7.3. Cognitive Accessibility and Clear Communication

AI-generated content should be understandable to its intended audience.

The platform should avoid unnecessarily complex language, long unstructured paragraphs and unexplained technical terminology.

Recommendations should be organised clearly, with relevant information presented in manageable sections. Participants should be able to request clarification or alternative formats.

For example, a job-matching result should not simply state that a vacancy is "compatible". It should explain which requirements appear aligned, which points require further discussion and which information is not yet available.

### 3.7.4. Accessible Participation and Human Support

Digital accessibility does not eliminate the need for human assistance.

Participants who cannot or do not wish to use a digital interface should retain access to appropriate support from FEMTalent advisers.

The platform should complement existing service channels and should not make access to employment guidance dependent on the ability to use a particular device, application or interaction method.

### 3.7.5. Co-Design and Accessibility Testing

Participants should be involved in the design and evaluation of accessibility features.

Testing should examine not only technical compliance but also practical usability, comfort, clarity and user confidence.

Feedback should be collected from participants with different accessibility requirements and used to prioritise improvements.

## 3.8. Social Impact Assessment and Evaluation

The social value of FEMConnect should be evaluated through a structured framework that combines operational indicators, participant feedback, accessibility evaluation and employment-related outcomes.

The evaluation should distinguish between the direct effects of the platform and broader employment outcomes that may be influenced by external factors, including labour-market conditions, employer decisions and individual circumstances.

### 3.8.1. Evaluation Principles

The evaluation framework should follow five principles:

1. **Participant-centred evaluation:** Assess whether the platform improves participants' experience, autonomy and access to opportunities.
2. **Evidence-based measurement:** Use defined indicators and documented measurement methods.
3. **Baseline comparison:** Establish current service performance before implementation.
4. **Fairness and inclusion:** Examine whether benefits and risks differ across participant groups, subject to appropriate legal and ethical safeguards.
5. **Continuous improvement:** Use evaluation results to modify workflows, system functionality and governance arrangements.

Numerical performance targets should be established only after baseline data and operational feasibility have been assessed.

### 3.8.2. Proposed Key Performance Indicators

The following indicators are proposed for a future pilot.

| Evaluation dimension   | Proposed indicator                                                             | Measurement approach                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Adviser efficiency     | Documentation time per participant                                             | Compare average time before and after implementation |
| Adviser capacity       | Time available for direct participant support                                  | Adviser time-use records                             |
| Service responsiveness | Time from initial intake to first vacancy discussion                           | Compare service records over defined periods         |
| Vacancy accessibility  | Share of reviewed vacancies with documented barrier analysis                   | Analyse completed vacancy assessments                |
| Participant autonomy   | Participant satisfaction with control over employment decisions                | Structured feedback and interviews                   |
| Recommendation quality | Proportion of recommendations accepted, modified or rejected after review      | Adviser review records                               |
| Matching usefulness    | Participant and adviser assessment of the relevance of suggested opportunities | Structured evaluation of sampled recommendations     |
| Training support       | Progress against participant-defined training objectives                       | Follow-up reviews                                    |
| Accessibility          | Usability and accessibility issues identified during testing                   | Accessibility audits and participant feedback        |
| Employer engagement    | Employer response to vacancy feedback and suggested improvements               | Employer follow-up records                           |
| Employment outcomes    | Employment placements and job stability at 6 and 12 months, where measurable   | Follow-up records and appropriate outcome analysis   |
| Responsible AI         | Privacy incidents, significant errors and documented corrective actions        | Incident and audit records                           |

These indicators should be interpreted carefully. For example, an increase in adviser overrides does not necessarily indicate poor system performance: it may demonstrate effective human oversight. The reasons for corrections and overrides are more informative than the rate alone.

Similarly, placement rates should not be treated as a direct measure of AI effectiveness without considering external influences and the characteristics of the participant population.

### 3.8.3. Baseline and Pilot Evaluation

Before deployment, FEMTalent should establish baseline measurements for its existing processes.

These should include:

- Average adviser time spent preparing participant documentation.
- Current time between participant intake and vacancy discussion.
- Existing methods for reviewing vacancy accessibility.
- Participant satisfaction with employment guidance.
- Current approaches to identifying training opportunities.
- Existing employer engagement and feedback processes.

A controlled pilot should then assess whether FEMConnect improves these processes.

The pilot should begin with a limited group of authorised users and a clearly defined set of workflows. It should use appropriately protected data and should not introduce automated exclusion or consequential decision-making.

Evaluation should combine quantitative indicators with qualitative interviews, user observations and structured feedback from participants, advisers and employers.

### 3.8.4. Monitoring Fairness and Unintended Consequences

The evaluation should specifically examine whether the platform creates or reinforces unequal outcomes.

Relevant questions include:

- Are some participants consistently presented with fewer employment opportunities?
- Are support needs being interpreted as negative indicators?
- Are recommendations overly concentrated in particular occupations?
- Are some accessibility requirements poorly supported?
- Are AI-generated summaries more error-prone for particular communication styles?
- Are advisers relying on recommendations differently across cases?
- Are participants able to challenge and correct inaccurate information?

Where sample sizes are small, results should be interpreted cautiously. The report should disclose limitations and avoid presenting weak statistical evidence as proof of fairness or absence of discrimination.

### 3.8.5. Social Impact Reporting

FEMTalent should periodically review evaluation findings and document:

- Improvements in service efficiency.
- Changes in participant experience and autonomy.
- Accessibility issues and corrective actions.
- Employer engagement and vacancy improvements.
- Recommendation quality and human overrides.
- Privacy, security and fairness incidents.
- Remaining limitations and future priorities.

The purpose of this reporting is to support organisational learning and accountability, not merely to demonstrate that the technology has been adopted.

## 3.9. Limitations and Outstanding Ethical Questions

Although FEMConnect has the potential to strengthen inclusive employment support, several limitations and open questions remain.

### 3.9.1. Dependence on Data Quality and Availability

The quality of AI-generated outputs will depend on the accuracy, completeness and relevance of available information.

Participant profiles may contain incomplete or outdated information. Vacancy descriptions may not accurately reflect actual workplace conditions. Training catalogues may not cover all available opportunities.

These limitations may affect the reliability of summaries, barrier assessments and recommendations.

The platform must therefore communicate uncertainty, preserve the distinction between missing and negative information, and allow users to update relevant records.

### 3.9.2. Limits of AI-Based Assessment

AI cannot fully understand an individual's aspirations, changing circumstances, lived experience or the practical realities of every workplace.

It may identify patterns and suggest possibilities, but it cannot independently determine whether a person will succeed in a particular job or whether a workplace adaptation will be effective.

The system should not be presented as an objective authority on employability, professional suitability or disability.

### 3.9.3. Risk of Reinforcing Existing Employment Inequalities

AI systems may reproduce historical patterns of exclusion even when disability-related information is removed.

For example, professional histories, employment gaps or previous job titles may indirectly reflect barriers experienced by people with disabilities.

Removing explicit health information is therefore not sufficient to guarantee fair outcomes. The platform must assess the broader effects of its recommendations and the assumptions embedded in its design.

### 3.9.4. Unresolved Data-Protection and Regulatory Questions

The final platform design must establish:

- Which personal-data categories are strictly necessary for each module.
- Which legal basis and, where applicable, Article 9 condition supports each processing purpose.
- Whether a DPIA is required and what risks it must address.
- Which external AI providers may process participant information.
- Whether data is transferred outside the European Economic Area and, if so, under what safeguards.
- Which modules qualify as high-risk under the AI Act.
- Which party is the provider, deployer or other responsible operator for each system component.
- Which Spanish and European employment-law requirements apply to the proposed workflows.

These questions cannot be resolved fully at the conceptual design stage. They require review of the final technical and organisational arrangements.

### 3.9.5. Participant Consent, Choice and Power Imbalances

Participants may feel pressure to accept AI-supported processes because they depend on employment services.

The organisation should therefore avoid treating consent as a simple formality. Where consent is used as a legal basis, its validity must be assessed in the specific circumstances, including whether it is freely given.

Participants should receive understandable information about the role of AI and should be able to ask questions, challenge outputs and access appropriate human support.

The platform should not make access to ordinary employment guidance unnecessarily dependent on agreeing to optional AI features.

### 3.9.6. Organisational and Resource Constraints

Responsible AI deployment requires more than purchasing or configuring software.

FEMTalent would need appropriate resources for system governance, technical maintenance, security, adviser training, accessibility testing, legal review and ongoing evaluation.

If these resources are insufficient, the organisation may be unable to provide meaningful oversight or maintain the quality of the service.

A phased implementation is therefore preferable to deploying all modules simultaneously.

### 3.9.7. Future Validation and Participant Involvement

The proposed ethical framework must be validated through direct engagement with the people affected by the platform.

Future work should include:

- Reviewing the existing interview evidence and validating the identified organisational bottlenecks with FEMTalent.
- Consulting participants with MS and other disabilities about their expectations and concerns.
- Testing accessibility requirements with people who use different assistive technologies.
- Consulting employment advisers about realistic human-oversight workflows.
- Discussing vacancy analysis and workplace adaptations with employers.
- Reviewing the data-protection and regulatory implications with qualified specialists.
- Evaluating the proposed safeguards during a limited pilot.

This process will help ensure that FEMConnect reflects actual service needs rather than assumptions made during the design phase.

### 3.9.8. Overall Ethical Position

The proposed FEMConnect ecosystem should be understood as a tool for improving access, support and informed decision-making, not as an automated employment-selection system.

Its success should not be measured only by administrative efficiency or the number of AI-generated recommendations. It should also be assessed according to whether participants retain autonomy, receive accessible support, gain access to meaningful opportunities and are protected against discriminatory or inappropriate use of their information.

The platform should be implemented only when its technical, organisational and legal safeguards are sufficiently mature.

**The central ethical commitment of FEMConnect is that AI should help remove barriers between people and employment opportunities, while preserving the dignity, rights, choices and individual potential of every participant.**
